The words behind the issue, explained simply.

Glossary

Some words on this site are technical, legal, or in Filipino. This glossary explains them in plain language, grouped by the page where they appear.

What is the Issue?

Words from the What is the Issue? page

Lupang Hinirang (Filipino: “chosen land”)
The official title and opening words of the Philippine national anthem, which describes the country as a land chosen and loved by its people. This site borrows it to show what is at stake.
Nilalason (Filipino: “being poisoned”)
Describes something that is slowly being poisoned. Used in this site’s title for land harmed by pollution.
Land pollution
The contamination or degradation of land and soil by waste, chemicals, or other harmful materials.
Soil degradation
A decline in soil quality, so it can no longer grow plants, store and filter water, or cycle nutrients as well as healthy soil.
Contaminant
Any substance that makes land, water, or air harmful or unfit for use, such as chemicals, oil, or heavy metals.
Heavy metals
Metals such as lead, mercury, and cadmium that are toxic even in small amounts. They can stay in soil for a long time and be absorbed by crops.
Agrochemicals
Chemicals used in farming, mainly pesticides and fertilisers. Too much or the wrong use can pollute soil and water.
Mine tailings
The crushed rock and processing waste left over after valuable minerals are taken out in mining. They may contain toxic chemicals.
Hazardous waste
Waste that can harm people or the environment because it is toxic, flammable, corrosive, or reactive, such as used oil and industrial chemicals.
Solid waste
Everyday discarded materials from homes, businesses, markets, and institutions, commonly called garbage or trash.
Unsegregated waste
Garbage that has been thrown out mixed together instead of being sorted into biodegradable, recyclable, residual, and special waste.
Open dumping
Throwing garbage on land without any controls to protect soil, water, and air. It is prohibited under Republic Act No. 9003.
Open burning
Burning garbage in the open air. It releases harmful smoke and is prohibited under Republic Act No. 9003.
Groundwater
Water stored underground in soil and rock. Many wells draw from it, so polluted soil can contaminate drinking water.
Biodiversity
The variety of living things in an area, including plants, animals, and microorganisms.
Projection
An estimate of a future number based on current data and trends, such as the projected amount of waste for 2025.
LGU (local government unit)
A province, city, municipality, or barangay. LGUs are responsible for collecting and managing waste in their area.
Barangay
The smallest unit of local government in the Philippines, similar to a village or district.
DENR-EMB
The Environmental Management Bureau of the Department of Environment and Natural Resources, the national office in charge of pollution control and solid waste management.
PSA
The Philippine Statistics Authority, the government agency that collects and publishes official statistics, including environment data.

What to do?

Words from the What to do? page

Iingatan (Filipino: “will be protected”)
Describes something that will be cared for and protected. Used in the title of the What to do? page.
Republic Act No. 9003
The Ecological Solid Waste Management Act of 2000, the main Philippine law on solid waste. It requires segregation, recycling, and composting, and bans open dumping and open burning.
Segregation at source
Sorting waste where it is produced, such as in the home, school, or office, before it is collected.
Biodegradable waste (also called compostable waste)
Waste that breaks down naturally, such as food scraps, fruit peels, and garden waste. It can be turned into compost.
Recyclable waste
Discarded materials that can be processed and made into new products, such as paper, cardboard, some plastics, glass, and metal cans.
Residual waste
Waste that can be neither recycled nor composted, such as used diapers and some plastic wrappers. It is the only kind that should go to a landfill.
Special waste
Household waste that needs special handling because it may be harmful, such as used batteries, paint, bulbs, and old electronics.
Materials recovery facility (MRF)
A place where collected waste is sorted. Under RA 9003, it includes a sorting station, drop-off center, composting area, and recycling area.
Composting
Turning biodegradable waste into compost, a natural soil conditioner, by letting it decompose under controlled conditions.
Sanitary landfill
A disposal site designed and run to keep waste from polluting soil, water, and air, for example with liners and covers. It replaces open dumpsites.
Waste diversion
Keeping waste out of landfills and dumpsites by reusing, recycling, or composting it. RA 9003 set a diversion target of at least 25% within five years.
National Solid Waste Management Commission (NSWMC)
The national body created by RA 9003 to oversee solid waste management and approve the waste plans of LGUs.
Ten-year solid waste management plan
A long-term plan every province, city, and municipality must prepare under RA 9003, setting targets for segregation, collection, recycling, and disposal.
Local solid waste management board
A group formed in each LGU to prepare and carry out its waste plan.
Zero waste
An approach that aims to reduce waste as much as possible and to reuse, recycle, or compost what is left, so almost nothing is dumped or burned.
Reuse and refill
A system where containers are used again instead of thrown away, for example buying soap or cooking oil in your own bottle at a refill store.
Junk shop
A local business that buys recyclable materials such as paper, bottles, and scrap metal and sells them to recyclers.

What Now?

Words from the What Now? page

Simulan Natin (Filipino: “let’s start”)
A call to begin acting together. Used in the title of the What Now? page.
Segregated collection
Collecting sorted waste separately, often on different days for each type, so that it is not mixed again in the garbage truck.
Clean-up drive
An organized activity where residents, students, or volunteers collect litter and dumped garbage from streets, creeks, and public areas.
Enforcement
Making sure laws are followed, for example by inspecting, warning, and fining people who litter or dump waste.
Ordinance
A local law passed by the council of a barangay, city, municipality, or province, such as a rule on segregation or plastic use.
Public reporting
When government offices regularly share their data and results with the public, so people can check whether programs are working.
